**Behavior > General** holds the three settings that define your agent's identity: its name, its role, and the instructions it follows on every turn. Role and Behavior together are the agent's system prompt.

## Assistant name

The project's display name in Agent Studio. It is not part of the prompt; if the agent should introduce itself by name, say so in Role.

## Role

A free-form field for who the agent is: the organization it represents and what it helps with. Keep it to one to three sentences and leave instructions for Behavior.

```text
You are the virtual assistant for Linden Hotels. You help guests with
reservations, amenities and directions for our twelve UK properties.
```

## Behavior

A free-form field for the instructions the agent needs on every turn: how it speaks, what is in and out of scope, when to use each tool, and how to handle problems and endings. Write it as short sections under markdown headings. The **Prompting guide** link next to the field opens the [prompting guide](/behavior/general/rules), which covers structure, a full example, and what to keep out of the prompt.

Behavior is not the place for facts (use [Knowledge](/knowledge/faqs/introduction)), multi-step processes (use [Flows](/flows/introduction)), or safety rules (use [Guardrails](/behavior/guardrails/introduction)).

## Where the other settings live

<Note>
  **Greeting.** The opening line is configured per channel, under **Voice > [Voice configuration](/voice-channel/advanced/call-settings)** or **Messaging > Advanced > Chat configuration**. It is spoken exactly as written, without the model. To set it from caller data at runtime, return an `utterance` from your [start tool](/tools/start-tool).
</Note>

| Setting | Where |
|---|---|
| Language and translations | **Behavior > Language** |
| Platform and custom guardrails | **Behavior > [Guardrails](/behavior/guardrails/introduction)** |
| Model | **Behavior > [Models](/behavior/models/model-use)**, set per channel |
| Voice, ambience, disclaimer | **Voice** settings |
