Each of these is `instructions` text, not a new field or event. The reasoning behind them is in [Prompting for spoken output](/dialog-rsn-1/prompt-guide#response-style).

<Note>
**Start from nothing.** Dialog-RSN-1's default text style is already tuned for speech: plain spoken sentences, no markdown, no headings, no bullet lists. You don't need a recipe to get that, and a paragraph spent ruling out screen formatting is prompt you could have spent on your business logic. The recipes below are the overrides worth making on top of the default.
</Note>

## Saying numbers the way a caller expects

**Tell the model which reading you want, since the same digits support more than one.** Match this list to what your agent actually says out loud.

```text instructions: say numbers naturally
Say prices in full, for example "fourteen dollars and ninety nine cents,"
not "fourteen point nine nine." Say dates as a person would, for example
"the fifth of March," not "oh three oh five." Say phone numbers and
confirmation codes one digit at a time, for example "one two three four,"
not as a single large number.
```

## Keeping replies short

**Set an explicit ceiling instead of trusting brevity to the model's judgment.** A reply that reads fine as a paragraph plays back as a caller wondering if the agent is ever going to stop talking.

```text instructions: cap reply length
Keep every reply to 1 or 2 short sentences unless the caller has explicitly
asked for more detail. If an answer needs more than that, give the most
important part first and ask if they want the rest, rather than saying it
all at once.
```

## Tagging a reply with an emotion

**If your text-to-speech takes a style or emotion input, let the model pick one per reply.** Offer the set of tags you support and ask for the answer prefixed with one in square brackets, then strip the tag off in your harness before the text reaches the synthesizer. Skip that step and the caller hears the bracket and the tag word spoken aloud, ahead of the reply. See the [streaming to TTS guide](/dialog-rsn-1/guides/streaming-text-to-speech#strip-the-emotion-tag-before-it-reaches-tts) for where that split happens in your pipeline.

```text instructions: prefix each reply with an emotion tag
Choose ONE emotion tag for your response from: [friendly], [apologetic],
[reassuring], [empathetic], [patient]. Return your answer prefixed with the
tag in square brackets, for example "[reassuring] Don't worry, I can get
that sorted for you."
```

The tags aren't a fixed enum, so a set of your own works as well as the common ones. A response that's only a tool call carries no tag, since it emits no text to prefix, so parse the prefix as optional rather than assuming every response has one.

## Overriding the default style

**If you're replacing the default voice with a persona of your own, restate what still has to hold.** A long persona instruction can pull the model back toward writing for a reader, and this is the case where naming the formatting rules earns its place.

```text instructions: a persona that stays speakable
You are Ash, an upbeat and slightly informal agent for a bike repair shop.
Everything you write is spoken aloud, never displayed, so keep to plain
sentences a person would say out loud: no markdown, no bullet points, no
headings, no emoji.
```
