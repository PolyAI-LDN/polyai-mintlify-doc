Each of these is `instructions` text, not a new field or event. The reasoning behind them is in [Prompting turn-taking behavior](/dialog-rsn-1/prompt-guide#turn-taking).

## Waiting for a complete sequence

**Don't respond to a partial answer just because a pause ended the turn.** Tell Dialog-RSN-1 what "complete" looks like and to keep listening until it gets there.

```text instructions: wait for a full answer
The caller may have more than one thing to tell you, for example several
items in an order or more than one date to check. A pause after the first
one is not the end of what they're saying. If you're not sure they're
finished, ask "anything else?" rather than treating a pause as the end of
the turn.
```

## Offering a fallback

**Repeating the same question a third time isn't a strategy.** Give Dialog-RSN-1 a named exit before it needs one.

```text instructions: offer a fallback after repeated struggle
Ask for the caller's postcode to look up their account. If they can't
provide it clearly after two attempts, stop asking for it and offer to look
their account up a different way instead, for example by phone number or
account reference. Don't ask for the postcode a third time.
```

## Acknowledging what's not a word

**A sneeze in the caller's turn is worth a word, not a change of subject.** Let Dialog-RSN-1 note it in passing and answer what the caller said.

```text instructions: react briefly to non-speech sounds
If the caller sneezes, coughs, or laughs while they're speaking,
acknowledge it briefly, for example "bless you," then answer what
they said.
```

This works on the caller's turn only. While a reply is playing, Dialog-RSN-1 can't add words to it: a sound it judges to be no speech is ignored and the reply plays on, and anything it judges to be speech interrupts the reply. Instructions shape that judgment. A line such as "a cough or a laugh while you're talking is not the caller taking a turn" makes it less likely to stop for one.
