A comparison shows exactly what is different between two versions of your agent, across knowledge, functions, flows, variants and settings. Use it before you publish, to confirm that only the change you intended is included.

## What you can compare

- **Your Branch against Live.** Shows what publishing will change.
- **Your Branch against the state it started from.** A snapshot is saved when a Branch is created, so you can see everything you have changed since.
- **Any two branches**, including a Sub-branch against its parent Branch.
- **Two versions in Live's history.** Shows what a past publish changed.

## Opening a comparison

You can start a comparison from three places:

- The **Publish** menu, in the branch you are working in.
- The compare icon on a branch card, on the **Deployments** page.
- The three dot menu on a branch, on the **Deployments** page.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose what to compare">
    Select two branches, or compare your Branch with the snapshot it started from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the differences">
    Changes are grouped by what they affect, so you can work through them in order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish or keep working">
    Exit the comparison to return to the editor, or publish once you are satisfied.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reading a comparison

Each change is marked by type:

- <Icon icon="plus" iconType="solid" color="#2D8C66" /> **Additions**, where a new item was added.
- <Icon icon="minus" iconType="solid" color="#D64F4F" /> **Deletions**, where an existing item was removed.
- <Icon icon="rotate" iconType="solid" color="#3B73D1" /> **Edits**, where an existing item was modified.

Every change names the person who made it and when. Use this to see who changed what when several people work in the same project, which also helps with compliance reviews in regulated industries.

## When to use it

- **Before you publish.** Confirm that nothing unexpected is included, particularly if your Branch has been open for a while.
- **Before you merge a colleague's Sub-branch.** Review their changes before they become part of the shared Branch.
- **When you find a Branch you did not expect.** See what is in it and who made the changes, then keep it or delete it.

A common case is an urgent fix. Someone creates a Branch from Live and makes the fix. Comparing it against Live confirms that nothing from an in-progress build has come with it. The comparison is how you prove the fix is isolated before it reaches customers.
